# Appendix intensity-threshold sensitivity without re-extraction

Reviewers asked for sensitivity on RI→intensity cut-points as well as discount parameters. We publish that stress test as an appendix twin of discount-parameter sensitivity: remap evidence-model intensity labels from stored mean relative improvements under one perturbed cut at a time (functional-suitability and resource/performance scales), hold the study set and discounted support masses fixed, re-run published-analogue Dempster–Shafer synthesis for headline effects, and leave forest plots, theory arrows, and `validated-synthesis.json` primary intensities untouched (ADR 0011). Aggregated intensity and belief measure may change when remapped focal elements are recombined; that is not re-discounting.

Non-reference cut values enter the grid only when literature shows at least moderate consensus on that magnitude role (currently: functional-suitability indifferent alternate **1%** near-lossless accuracy band; resource/performance strong alternate **75%** from 4× INT8-vs-FP32 memory). Arithmetic ±1 grids and single-source bands (e.g. 3%, 30%) are excluded; cuts without a consensus alternate are not perturbed. Citation trail: `.scratch/intensity-threshold-sensitivity/literature-cut-points.md`.

**Considered options.** Re-extracting intensities inside papers under alternate thresholds would mix encoding changes with extraction judgment and is not reproducible from processed artifacts. Re-discounting when intensities move would confound the RI→intensity encoding stress test with belief-assignment parameters already covered by the discount-parameter appendix. Regenerating forest plots or feeding cut-point results into intensity reconciliation would imply an alternate primary encoding; we reject that so the appendix cannot silently drift the manuscript authorities. Inventing a dense cut grid without multi-source corroboration would overclaim literature support for threshold subjectivity stress tests.
